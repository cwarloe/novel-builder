# STEP 6 — long synopsis (pilot)
Only locked facts. Options in brackets. No chapter numbers.

Tommy goes to Lincoln Middle School in a pre-smartphone now (some year 2008–2012). He is not the kid the hall makes room for. A pack finds him between classes. They use his name and his body. A bell saves him. At lunch he sits with Mark, who was also alone, phone on the table. The pack finds that too. Mark says leave him alone and then folds when they know his street. A teacher’s walk ends it. Tommy bikes home. A car of older boys clips the air around him. In his room: laptop, comics, a science-fiction book he has already finished, Legos. David stands in the doorway and asks how school was. Tommy says school is school, then that Dad does not understand this world. David offers chess club, tournaments, the presidency he once had. Tommy cannot use that tool. Jen calls them to dinner. Afterward she wants the counselor or the teacher. David does not fight her. Tommy builds until the house is quiet.

Mark has already found an antique shop in old town. They ride there. In a case is a watch with a mark on the face. Tommy cannot buy it. [OPTION: Mark pays. OPTION: Mark takes it. OPTION: he almost takes it and then pays.] Later the watch is on Tommy’s wrist in the house. Jen and David see it. The hallway will see it too and call it a couple gift or a stolen gift. Neither reading is Mark’s reason. He wanted Tommy to have one object the pack did not hand him.

They both lean on the button. The room is 1993.

Cars, prices, mouths, and the absence of a pocket internet mark them before they speak. They walk until they find a kid in the same shape of trouble Tommy knows from Lincoln. The kid has David’s face, younger. He does not know them. Chess may already be the hole he hides in. Tommy moves toward the beating. He cannot finish it. 1993 does not take a speech from a boy in the wrong sneakers.

Night comes. They are gone from Jen’s kitchen. [OPTION A: a motel takes cash. OPTION B: a desk turns them away; they sleep unpaid. OPTION C: only if they already know Davey’s house — yard or garage, not a first beat.] In NOW, Jen tracks time. David does not know what year his son is standing in.

Morning is still 1993 until the button. Then breakfast. Backpack. Jen’s face. David’s doorway voice, which will misread the look as mood. Tommy has a fact he cannot say cleanly: Dad was that kid. He is not braver. He is less able to pretend David came from nowhere. Mark knows the watch did not buy safety. It bought a night they have to lie about. Davey remains in 1993, still a kid. How adult David ever knows the visitors is not required for this synopsis.

The book from here is Monday at Lincoln with that fact in the house — not a redeemed bully, not a purpose-of-time-travel, not an estranged-dad reunion.
