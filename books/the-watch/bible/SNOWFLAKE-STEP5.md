# STEP 5 — POV synopses (one night lock)

Each is the story as that person lives it. No new IDs.

## Tommy
Lincoln takes him apart in pieces (hall, cafeteria, bike). David offers chess club. Jen offers dinner and a look he cannot answer. Mark offers a ride to old town and then a watch Tommy cannot pay back. Hallway reads the gift as a joke or a couple. They both lean on the button and the room is 1993. Prices, mouths, cars are wrong. He sees a kid who has David’s face in the same shape of trouble. He moves toward it and cannot finish it. Night happens somewhere unpaid or barely paid; he does not sleep like a person who will be believed at home. Morning is still 1993 until the button. Breakfast in NOW: backpack, Jen’s face, David’s doorway voice. He has a fact he cannot say cleanly. He is not braver. He is less able to pretend Dad came from nowhere.

## Mark
He is the one who found the shop and can produce the object. He wants Tommy to have a win the hallway cannot give. The gift is too big. People will call it romance, charity, or theft; only one of those might be materially true and he has not had to choose yet. He says should we. He leans. 1993 looks at him with NOW clothes and a Black face in a town we have not named. Cash becomes a problem at a desk or on a street. He can joke until someone asks for money or a name. He does not get to be only the sidekick. After the night he knows the watch did not buy Tommy safety. It bought a night they have to lie about.

## David
He lives here. He asks how was school. He tells the chess story because it is the tool that once worked. Jen wants a counselor; he does not fight her. He sees a watch on his son’s wrist and files it under friend. That night the house is short one boy, or two if Mark was expected to have gone home. He does not know 1993 is happening. Morning: Tommy is back with a look David will misread as mood. The book does not require David to remember two visitors yet. His change starts as unease — the advice bounced harder than usual — and can become recognition later without this synopsis forcing it.

## Jen
She feeds people and converts fear to a call. She sees the watch, the untouched plate, the “I’m fine.” The night they are gone is her plot: time, phone, whether to wake David again. She does not jump. Morning she will take a body count and a weak story. She remains the person who might actually call Lincoln.
