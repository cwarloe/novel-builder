The locker row was already loud when he got there. Someone had a sneaker squeaking on purpose. Someone else laughed at nothing. Tommy kept his backpack on one shoulder so he could turn if he had to.

They were in the path. Not a name he was allowed to use. Three of them, maybe four. The tall one tapped the locker next to Tommy’s with a knuckle, like he was knocking on a door that belonged to him.

“Look who showed up,” the tall one said.

Tommy reached for the lock. His fingers felt thick. He did not swing. He did not have a sentence.

A shoulder found his. Not hard enough for a teacher to call it anything. Hard enough that the lock jumped under his hand. He muttered it without looking up.

“Leave me alone.”

They used his name then, the way kids use a name when they have decided it is funny. One of them stretched the first syllable. Tommy’s mouth went dry. He stared at the metal vent in the locker door until the squares blurred.

The bell saved him. It was ugly and too close and it scattered them like it had a job. The tall one knocked the locker one more time on the way past, a little goodbye.

Tommy got the door open. Books. The zipper on the backpack that always caught. He was intact. He was smaller. He walked with the crowd because the crowd was moving, and that was enough to be a person who had a class to get to.

He did not look back.
